# Introduction to the Snow

## Or, the story of the Trial of the Snow

Anna woke up in an unfamiliar hallway, her head throbbing. It was dark as she laid on her back, pain blurring her vision. "Where am I?". This didn't _feel_ like Mythra, or any of the Multi-world. It felt unfamiliar, yet familiar. She stumbled blindly to her feet, slipping a bit as her feet found purchase. She walked forward, the pain in her shoulder causing her to stop. Darkness continued as slowly but surely Anna continued, her feet— bare, she noted— falling on cold stone. She continued to walk, soon seeing a golden light in front of her. The light came back into her eyes, knowing only celestial beings could create such bright, shining lights. But soon, her vision faded as she began to feel the effects of her serious injuries. She, of course, had been aware of the injuries, but it dawned on her that she might have fallen far, but did not know _exactly_ how far. She remembered falling, but now her mind was elsewhere, just as her body was.

She continued into the light despite her fatigue. Soon, she came to a wall, a tarnished mirror waiting.

Over her shoulders hung a white cape embroidered with roses. Four clusters of diamonds gleamed upon it, though only two burned with light. The others lay dark, waiting. She felt, without knowing why, that they marked something missing—steps of a quest she had already begun.

Beneath the cape, her body was wrapped in plain beige cloth. It was simple, almost blank, as though her former self had been stripped away to make room for something new.

But when her gaze rose again, the changes grew stranger still. Her face was no longer flesh. Stone stared back at her, smooth and unyielding. Her right eye was a narrow crescent, blinking with alien patience. Over her left eye, a wound festered—dark, rootlike matter spreading across her vision, alive and invasive. It mirrored the fracture she felt within herself.

Her hands trembled as she lifted them. They too were stone—scuffed, yet smooth, reflecting the light of the cape’s diamonds. They were not broken, but reforged. Tools of action, waiting.

She continued on, slowing down more as her injuries took her. The darkness continued as she stumbled on, her pain growing, her groans getting fainter and more frequent as she delved deeper into the hall. She didn't know where she was going, but she knew she would get there eventually.

Suddenly, after a agonizing half-hour— or perhaps more— Anna saw more bright light. Cold and near death, she pressed towards it. Multiple times, she stumbled. Multiple times, she fell, only to get up back again, the pain spreading. As her vision returned as she reached the light, a big room made its presence— and its warmth— known.

It felt so good to finally see a clearing, after walking in a hallway so long. A single flame in a torch stood in the middle of the room, and Anna gratefully took her hands of stone and put them to the flame. Her pain began to fade, and she realized— was this light _healing_ her?

She decided, on a whim, to check her cape. More of the clusters of the diamonds lining the middle of her flower-printed cape were lit. _Huh, useful_, she thought, her normal mental faculties returning to full operation as the light filled her with power. She turned away from the torch to survey her location. It was in fact a clearing in what until now had been a single, long hallway, but it wasn't stone. It was crystals, a deep aquamarine and purple, slightly shining from the light both from Anna's cape and the torch. Anna looked down to see the floor was made of shining stone. It pulsated with the same colors that the walls had on them, but also— the pulsing seemed to direct her… to the left? She looked back up at the wall beside her as she continued, warily, stopping at various torches and wall sconces as she felt the pain come back. Something was hurting her, but she couldn't tell what.

Soon, she found darkness and a dead end. Her arms had started hurting again, their damage increased by this phantom force. She reversed course, again stopping at every light along the way. But, to her dismay, she found that there was a dead end that was not illuminated. She turned around, confused. Certainly this wasn't meant to… kill  her? But her confusion deepened when she returned to where the mirror had been. In its stead was another path, one that had not been there before. She followed it, confused, and soon came to another wall, thankfully with a torch on its rear side. Recharging, she thought upon the seemingly magic powers that this light seemed to carry. It could heal whatever this… this _poison_ that she had, without question. But, what was this pain? It was then she noticed that her skin was a darker tan then it normally was.

As she noted this, she reached _another_ dead end. "The hell?" Anna was distraught at this dead end, different than the last, which in turn was different from every other one. At this point she had seen more dead ends than clearings, and she was getting quite tired about it. Each dead end seemed to explain the futility of its beholder, and how she _could_ have made different decisions, but chose not to. With each clearing she found, the lamp's position shifted slightly, slightly different colors, different textures, different _feelings_ attached to it, all different sounds, all voices chiding her. The walls all seemed to point at Anna, taunting her, keeping her stuck in this cyclic routine of confusion. The floor continued to misdirect, _laugh_ at her, with many voices overlapping in its colors, its nonchalance grating yet purposeful and kind.

She was getting crabbier by the minute. Anna was never one to wait around and wander like this, trace circles over. She was confused by the _constant_ dead ends, and the headache hadn't helped at all either. Finally, after another thirty minutes, Anna found another clearing with another light source. She refilled her… pain tolerance? She was still confused by how that worked. Did the light fill some unseen reservoir of power within her? She finally accepted this as the truth, for _none_ of this made _any_ sense.
